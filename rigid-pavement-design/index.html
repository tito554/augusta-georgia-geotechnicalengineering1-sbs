Augusta Georgia sits on the Piedmont Plateau, a region of deep residual soils weathered from crystalline rock. The clayey sands and silts common here pose unique challenges for rigid pavement design. High plasticity in some layers, coupled with a shallow water table near the Savannah River, demands thorough subgrade characterization. We integrate subgrade CBR testing directly into our pavement design workflow to set accurate modulus values for the concrete slab. Without this local data, standard AASHTO assumptions can lead to premature cracking or excessive thickness. Our team has completed over forty rigid pavement projects across Augusta Georgia, ranging from residential streets to heavy industrial access roads. We know the soil variability block by block.

Optimizing rigid pavement design in Augusta Georgia requires local subgrade data — generic AASHTO defaults miss the Piedmont soil variability.

Scope of work

For a recent distribution center off I-20, we encountered a perched water table at 1.8 meters depth. The rigid pavement design required a 28-day flexural strength of 4.5 MPa and a k-value of 55 MPa/m from the subgrade reaction test. We ran plate load tests and correlated them with dynamic cone penetrometer profiles.
  • Slab thickness optimized between 200 mm and 280 mm depending on traffic class
  • Joint spacing set at 4.5 m for plain concrete with dowel bars at transverse joints
  • Subbase layer specified as 150 mm of cement-stabilized sand
This approach cut concrete volume by 12% compared to the initial design. We also included a geotechnical instrumentation plan to monitor slab deflection during the first year of service. The owner approved the design after reviewing the risk-cost tradeoff.

Area-specific notes

The residual soils of Augusta Georgia often contain relict joints and slickensides from the parent rock structure. These features reduce the effective subgrade modulus locally, creating differential support under the slab. In our experience, ignoring this anisotropy causes corner cracking within two years. The Savannah River floodplain adds another layer of risk: shallow groundwater can weaken the subgrade during spring rains. We always model saturated conditions in the rigid pavement design to ensure the slab performs under worst-case moisture scenarios. A simple drainage layer beneath the subbase is a low-cost fix that prevents long-term pumping and faulting.

How does the Piedmont soil affect rigid pavement performance in Augusta Georgia?

Piedmont residual soils in Augusta Georgia typically have moderate to high plasticity and variable density due to relict rock structure. This causes non-uniform support under the slab. We mitigate this by specifying a cement-stabilized subbase layer and using a conservative k-value from plate load tests. Joint spacing is reduced to 4.5 m to control curling stresses.

Can you design a rigid pavement for a parking lot with heavy truck traffic?

Yes. We design rigid pavements for parking lots, loading docks, and industrial yards in Augusta Georgia. For heavy truck traffic, we increase concrete flexural strength to 5.0 MPa and add dowel bars at all transverse joints. We also evaluate the need for a lean concrete subbase to prevent subgrade pumping under frequent loads.
