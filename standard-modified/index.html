Proctor Test in Augusta Georgia: Standard & Modified Compaction

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

Many contractors in Augusta Georgia assume that running a standard Proctor test on any borrow source is enough to satisfy compaction specs. That shortcut can backfire when the modified Proctor becomes mandatory for deep fills or heavy-traffic subgrades. The two methods differ in compactive effort — 12,400 ft-lbf/ft³ for standard versus 56,250 ft-lbf/ft³ for modified — and choosing the wrong one leaves you with a target density that doesn't match field roller performance. We routinely see specs that call for 95% of modified Proctor maximum dry density, yet the lab receives a sample tested only to ASTM D698. Before you compact a single lift, pair the correct Proctor method with subgrade CBR testing to confirm design strength, or use Atterberg limits to flag moisture-sensitive soils that shift optimum water content.

Standard Proctor at 12,400 ft-lbf/ft³ suits light fills; modified at 56,250 ft-lbf/ft³ matches heavy rollers on high-traffic subgrades.

Scope of work

In Augusta Georgia the Savannah River alluvium and residual Piedmont clays behave very differently under the hammer. We have run enough Proctor curves here to know that a silty sand from the Aiken Plateau may hit 120 pcf at 10% moisture under modified effort, while a fat clay from south of Gordon Highway barely reaches 100 pcf. Our lab runs both methods side by side when the project involves multiple borrow sources. The procedure follows ASTM D698 (standard) or ASTM D1557 (modified) with a 4-inch or 6-inch mold depending on particle size. We record moisture-density curves, zero air voids line, and report optimum moisture content with maximum dry density. For projects that also require strength parameters we coordinate with direct shear testing on remolded specimens, and when the fill is intended for retaining wall backfill we check geotextile separation compatibility at the same moisture range.

Area-specific notes

The difference in soil behavior between downtown Augusta and the suburban developments west of I-20 is striking. Downtown fills often sit on old terrace deposits with high silt content — these soils are forgiving under standard Proctor but can collapse under modified energy, producing a much lower optimum moisture and higher density. Out near Evans, the residual clays derived from granite gneiss are so plastic that a 2% shift in water content can drop dry density by 8 pcf. If a project specifies modified Proctor but the lab runs standard, the contractor will over-compact trying to hit a target that is physically unreachable with that soil. That leads to pump failures, shearing in the fill, and costly rework. We always cross-check the specified method with the borrow source geology.

Q&A

What is the difference between standard and modified Proctor tests?

The difference is compactive effort. Standard Proctor (ASTM D698) applies 12,400 ft-lbf/ft³ using a 5.5 lb hammer dropped 12 inches in 3 layers. Modified Proctor (ASTM D1557) applies 56,250 ft-lbf/ft³ using a 10 lb hammer dropped 18 inches in 5 layers. The higher effort in modified produces a higher maximum dry density and a lower optimum moisture content, which better simulates heavy rollers used on highway and airfield projects.

How much does a Proctor test cost in Augusta Georgia?

The typical range for a Proctor test in Augusta Georgia is between US$90 and US$200 per sample, depending on whether you need standard or modified effort, and whether the material requires a 6-inch mold for oversize particles. Volume discounts apply for multiple borrow sources tested at the same time.

When do I need a modified Proctor instead of a standard Proctor?

You need a modified Proctor when the project specification calls for 95% or 98% of modified maximum dry density — common on Georgia DOT highway projects, airport pavements, and heavy industrial slabs. Standard Proctor is acceptable for most residential subdivisions, light commercial parking lots, and general site grading where the roller weight is under 10 tons.
