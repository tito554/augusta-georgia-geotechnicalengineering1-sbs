Laboratory Permeability Test (Falling/Constant Head) in Augusta Georgia

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

The Savannah River floodplain and the Fall Line transition zone give Augusta Georgia a unique mix of sandy terrace deposits and residual clay soils over crystalline bedrock. For any project that involves groundwater flow—whether it is a foundation excavation, a retaining wall drainage system, or a stormwater infiltration basin—knowing the hydraulic conductivity of the soil is not optional. We run laboratory permeability tests following ASTM D2434 for coarse-grained materials and ASTM D5084 for fine-grained specimens, using either the constant-head or falling-head method depending on the soil type. The data we generate feeds directly into seepage analyses and dewatering plans, and it is often the deciding factor between a dry excavation and a costly mud problem. Before we start, we usually cross-check field conditions with a permeability test in the field to calibrate our lab results against in-situ behavior.

A single order-of-magnitude error in permeability can double your dewatering costs or cause a retaining wall to fail from hydrostatic pressure.

Scope of work

A common mistake among contractors in Augusta Georgia is assuming that all sandy soils drain at the same rate. The truth is that the Coastal Plain sediments here often contain interbedded silt lenses that reduce permeability by an order of magnitude, and the residual Piedmont clays can behave like aquitards even when they look porous. Our laboratory permeability test addresses this by measuring the actual coefficient of permeability (k) under controlled conditions. For clean sands and gravels we apply the constant-head method, which circulates water through the specimen until steady-state flow is reached. For silts and clays we use the falling-head method, where the drop in water level over time gives us the k-value. Both procedures are run on undisturbed or remolded samples, and we always record the test temperature to correct for viscosity. The result is a number that tells you exactly how fast water moves through that soil, which is critical when you are sizing underdrains or designing slope drainage. We also coordinate with our ensayo SPT to correlate permeability with blow counts in the same borehole.

Area-specific notes

Augusta grew as a river port and manufacturing hub, and much of its industrial and commercial infrastructure sits on fill placed over the original floodplain. These man-made deposits have highly variable permeability: one area may be compacted sandy fill that drains well, while ten feet away you hit loose silty fill that holds water for days. If you rely on a single hand-auger sample to design your drainage layer, you risk underestimation of groundwater inflow during excavation or overestimation of infiltration capacity for a detention basin. A laboratory permeability test on multiple samples from the same site gives you a spatial picture of how water moves through the ground, so you can avoid the kind of post-construction drainage failures that plague older developments along the riverfront.

Q&A

How much does a laboratory permeability test cost in Augusta Georgia?

A standard falling-head or constant-head test costs between $360 and $720 per sample, depending on the method and number of gradient stages. Prices may vary if the sample requires special handling or if multiple readings are needed for a custom variable-gradient test.

Which method is better for the sandy soils near the Savannah River?

Constant-head is the right choice for clean sands and gravels found in the river terrace and floodplain deposits. Falling-head is reserved for silty sands or clays. We decide after reviewing the grain size distribution from a prior sieve analysis.

Can you run a permeability test on a disturbed sample?

Yes, but the results will represent the remolded permeability, not the in-situ value. For undisturbed specimens we use thin-walled Shelby tubes. If only bag samples are available, we compact them to the estimated field density and report the remolded k with a clear note.

How long does it take to get results?

Constant-head tests take 2 to 3 days from sample receipt. Falling-head tests on low-permeability clays can take 5 to 7 days because the flow rate is very slow. We provide preliminary numbers within 48 hours if requested for an urgent job.
