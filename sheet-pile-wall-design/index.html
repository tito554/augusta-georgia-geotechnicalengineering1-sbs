Sheet Pile Wall Design in Augusta Georgia

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

In Augusta, the Savannah River floodplain means we often find loose alluvial sands and soft clays down to 10 m depth before hitting the dense micaceous sands of the Coastal Plain. Designing a sheet pile wall here requires precise knowledge of the lateral earth pressures, the water table elevation, and the driving resistance of the bearing stratum. We combine the results of our local ensayo SPT profiles with pore pressure measurements to define the effective stress parameters for each soil layer. That direct correlation between blow counts and friction angles is what allows us to select the correct section modulus for the wall.

Scope of work

A recent project along the Augusta Canal involved a 9 m deep excavation for a pump station. The client needed a cantilevered sheet pile wall that could resist both active soil pressure and hydrostatic uplift. We ran a 2D finite element analysis using Plaxis 2D, modeling the wall as a plate element with interface friction. The key was to limit lateral deflection to 25 mm at the top. To handle the high water table, we specified a tieback system combined with anclajes grouted into the lower sand unit. The design also required a scour analysis because the wall sits within 15 m of the canal bank. For the upper 4 m of soft clay, we recommended a estabilización de taludes treatment to reduce long-term creep movement behind the wall.

Area-specific notes

The primary geotechnical risk in Augusta Georgia is underseepage and piping through the alluvial sands during excavation. According to IBC Section 1806 and ASCE 7 Chapter 17, the design flood elevation must be considered for any permanent wall within the 100-year floodplain. If the sheet pile wall does not penetrate at least 1.5 m into the dense lower sand, the hydraulic gradient can exceed the critical value of 0.5, triggering backward erosion. We always verify the embedment depth using the Terzaghi-Peck method for seepage control, and we include a filter layer behind the wall to relieve excess pore pressure. Failure to account for this has led to several cofferdam collapses in the region.

Q&A

What soil parameters are needed for sheet pile wall design in Augusta Georgia?

We need the unit weight, effective friction angle, and cohesion from triaxial tests on the alluvial sands and clays. The water table depth is critical, as is the blow count profile from an SPT test to estimate the relative density of the lower sand. We also require the Atterberg limits for the clay layers to assess plasticity.

How deep does a sheet pile wall need to be embedded in Augusta soils?

The embedment depth depends on the retained height, soil strength, and water table. For a typical 6 m excavation in the Savannah River floodplain, we usually embed the wall 3 to 4 m into the dense lower sand. The embedment is verified using the Terzaghi-Peck method for seepage control and the Blum method for structural stability.

What is the typical cost range for sheet pile wall design in Augusta?

The design fee typically ranges between US$1,380 and US$4,550, depending on the excavation depth, number of tieback rows, and the complexity of the flood load analysis. This includes the soil parameter review, the 2D finite element modeling, and the preparation of construction drawings.

How do you handle the high water table in Augusta for sheet pile walls?

We specify a filter layer of granular material behind the wall to relieve pore pressure, and we size the weep holes at 1.5 m spacing. For permanent walls, we design a drainage system that outlets below the flood elevation. The wall embedment is checked against the hydraulic gradient to prevent piping.
